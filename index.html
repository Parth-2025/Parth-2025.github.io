KinetiQ

Upload a video of your swing, shot, or throw — KinetiQ breaks your form down phase by phase and tells you exactly what to fix, using pose estimation instead of a coach.

Walkthrough: upload a video → KinetiQ isolates the phases of the motion → scores each one and flags what to fix.

A custom phase-detection algorithm isolates these five biomechanical phases from a single video and scores joint angles against ideal ranges for each one.

React Native FastAPI MediaPipe Firebase Auth0
The hard part

The difficult part of creating this app was the integration of MediaPipe into this software. Determining the pose estimations was extremely compute-heavy, and under a real load the backend would skip frames or stall completely. I had to redesign the architecture to be able to have a system to split the uploaded videos into snapshots every so many seconds to identify the frame with the pose detection.

What I learned

KinetiQ taught me more about what can break and what worked. I spent days addressing scaling failures, as it was one of my biggest issues. Under a real load, MediaPipe would skip frames or stall completely. I had to go back and redesign the architecture to cleanly distribute processing evenly. Another issue I faced was Auth0. This was my first time using it, and many times the small issues ended up being the hardest issues to track and resolve: tokens needed to be refreshed properly, wiring the callback URLs correctly, and making sure Auth0 persisted in the app correctly. None of it was conceptually hard; however, it was a real reminder that integrating a new tool always costs more time than expected.

The lesson that stuck with me afterward was that a feature working in a demo versus production are two different problems altogether, and I did not understand that gap until I hit it myself.

SCENE 03
What I'm building now

Bite Right

Bite Right came out of watching friends and family ask a waiter the same questions at every meal — “Are there peanuts in this?” or “Is there any dairy in the sauce?” — and hope the answer was right. Often the staff didn't actually know what was in the sauces or marinades. Eating out on a restricted diet shouldn't come with that much uncertainty.

Food item detector for allergic people prior to consumption. Upload an image of the food product or label, and Bite Right analyzes it for you, giving you an output of everything you should avoid according to your conditions without having to go through the label yourself. The current detection success rate is 95%.

~35-second walkthrough: upload a food photo → Bite Right checks it against your restrictions → flags what's risky.

What would move this forward

The current version runs on rule-based detection logic. The next step is training a proper model to get more accurate and handle a wider range of foods and restrictions — that's what I'm working on now.

What I learned: I first modeled coaches and students as two separate collections, so every booking meant stitching data across both and the queries got ugly fast. Github_PRReview

A code-change-risk agent: builds an import graph for a repo, scores how far a pull request's changes could ripple, and uses an LLM to separate real dead code from false positives. Built for a Palantir AIP submission.

What I learned: the pure static import-graph pass flagged tons of "dead" code that wasn't — dynamic imports, reflection, framework entry points. The graph alone wasn't trustworthy. Layering an LLM verification step on top of it was what made the output something a reviewer would actually act on, and it changed how I think about when to reach for static analysis vs. a model.
